# Case studies

Worked examples of how statements become Validatorium records. Each case takes
a statement as someone would actually say it and shows its decomposition into
atomic premises, the premises it depends on without saying so, the scope it
leaves out, and the kind of evidence each part needs.

These are illustrations, not specification. The rules they follow are in
`docs/specs/` (in particular `data-model.md`, `premise-decomposition.md` and
`argdown-conventions.md`).

| Case | What it shows |
|---|---|
| [Jeff's favorite food](jeffs-favorite-food.md) | One casual sentence carries at least nine premises, rooted in "there is a person named Jeff": self-referential preferences, a premise about a product's history, and unstated premises about time, manufacturer and experience. |
